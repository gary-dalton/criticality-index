# Distorted SOC Signatures — A Detection Catalog

## Purpose

This document catalogs the observable signatures that distinguish natural Self-Organized Criticality from distorted SOC regimes. It is a **detection specification**, not a theoretical framework: it defines what to measure, what co-occurrence patterns to look for, and how to discriminate between candidate regimes. The *mechanism* producing any observed pattern (overtopping, liquefaction, or something else) is a separate question, addressed in the mechanism-specific docs (`overtopping.md`, `liquefaction.md`).

Two detection categories are defined:

- **CSOC-like signature** — the bundle of observables produced when small events are systematically suppressed, with accumulated deficit released as disproportionately large events. Superficially resembles a subcritical system.
- **ISOC-like signature** — the bundle of observables produced when cascades are amplified mid-propagation, with elevated activity and history-dependent response. Superficially resembles a supercritical system.

These are empirical categories. A system showing a CSOC-like signature may be an overtopping system, a suppressed-release system of another type, or something the framework has not yet enumerated. The detection category names what is observed; the mechanism question comes next.

---

## Part I: The Evidentiary Standard

> **The confirmation threshold for a distorted SOC signature is multiple independent tests being simultaneously consistent.**

No single observable confirms a signature category. Each individual test can arise from non-critical or non-distorted mechanisms. The strength of the claim scales with the convergence of independent lines of evidence across observational domains.

Categories in this catalog are specified as **bundles** — a named pattern requires the co-occurrence of a defined set of observations within defined statistical tolerances. The bundle definitions (Part II and Part III) specify which observations must co-occur, which are diagnostic, and which are merely consistent with the category.

## Part II: The CSOC-Like Signature Bundle

A system exhibits a CSOC-like signature when the following observations co-occur within statistical tolerance:

### II.1 Distributional signatures

- **Power law truncated at the low end** — small event tail is suppressed below some characteristic scale
- **Excess large events** — above that scale, more large events than natural SOC predicts
- **Characteristic scale appears** — at the suppression threshold, introducing a scale that true SOC should not have
- **Exponent relations fail** — scaling relations between distribution exponents are violated
- **Avalanche shape collapse fails** — small and large event populations are generated by qualitatively different dynamics and do not rescale onto a common profile
- **xmin-bracket widening** — the gap between α(xmin=5) and α(xmin=10) at fixed L is a cheap detection signal. In natural SOC, the bracket is narrow (~0.013 on 2D Manna at L=1024). Suppression distorts the small-event region preferentially: xmin=5 gets increasingly biased by the truncated low-s tail, while xmin=10 is less affected. Bracket widening to ≳0.05 at the same L is a CSOC-like fingerprint even before any other diagnostic runs. **Caveat in real data:** under-reporting of small events produces a superficially similar bracket widening without any physical suppression. Use this signal in combination with redistribution-dependent signatures (spectral knee, non-stationary σ, quasi-periodicity) rather than alone. See `../validation/01_04_manna_overtopping.md` Model B/C measurement battery and [`real_data_considerations.md`](real_data_considerations.md) for the full distinguishability analysis.

### II.2 Spectral / fractal signatures

- **1/f spectrum develops a knee** — flat or white-noise-like at low frequencies, transitioning to 1/f at higher frequencies
- **Self-similarity breaks at fine scales** — fractal dimension inconsistent across scales
- **Characteristic frequency appears** — corresponding to the suppression threshold
- **Long-range temporal correlations distorted** — small events that maintain them are preferentially removed
- **Hurst exponent altered** from natural SOC baseline

### II.3 Network / graph-theoretic signatures

- **Weak edges functionally removed** — small events often represent weak-connection activations
- **Degree distribution truncated** — low-degree nodes become functionally invisible
- **Small-world property degrades** — short path lengths depend on weak long-range connections that suppression preferentially removes
- **Network fragments toward modularity** — without weak inter-module connections, the graph breaks into more isolated clusters
- **Global efficiency drops** — locally efficient but globally poor
- **Hub dependence increases** — over-reliance on strong high-degree connections

### II.4 Information-theoretic signatures

- **Entropy reduced** — accessible state space is truncated
- **Mutual information degraded** — particularly at fine scales
- **Dynamic range compressed** — lower end of discriminable inputs is lost
- **Transfer entropy distorted** — dominated by large events; fine-grained continuous transfer is lost
- **Fisher information peak shifts or broadens** — system no longer at maximum sensitivity

### II.5 Dynamical signatures

- **Branching ratio σ appears subcritical** during accumulation phase
- **σ appears supercritical** during and immediately following large release events
- **σ is non-stationary** across the full cycle
- **Susceptibility suppressed** during accumulation, spiking before large releases
- **Inter-event intervals quasi-periodic** for large events — a characteristic period absent in natural SOC
- **Deep quiescence post-release** followed by gradual resumption of small events
- **Slow baseline drift** in activity measures between large events

### II.6 The unifying pattern

> Across all domains, suppression of small events consistently produces the same fundamental distortion: **a characteristic scale is introduced where there should be none, and the system loses its capacity to operate across the full range of scales simultaneously.**

## Part III: The ISOC-Like Signature Bundle

A system exhibits an ISOC-like signature when the following observations co-occur within statistical tolerance:

### III.1 Distributional signatures

- **Power law distorted at the large end** — excess of large events beyond natural SOC prediction
- **Small event frequency elevated** in absolute terms but **reduced relative to large events**
- **Characteristic scale may appear** at the amplification threshold
- **Exponent relations fail** — the inflated distribution breaks scaling consistency
- **Avalanche shape collapse fails** — amplified propagation produces event profiles that do not collapse cleanly with natural SOC events
- **Mean event size elevated** relative to natural SOC baseline
- **Apparent τ shallower** than the natural SOC value for the system's universality class

### III.2 Spectral / fractal signatures

- **1/f spectrum steepened or distorted** — excess power at low frequencies corresponding to large events
- **Spectral knee at the amplification scale** — below the amplification threshold the spectrum may appear different from above it
- **Self-similarity breaks at coarse scales** — large events dominate and distort the fractal structure
- **Long-range temporal correlations altered** — reflecting amplification dynamics rather than natural SOC
- **Hurst exponent elevated** — persistent long-range correlations from amplified large events rather than natural SOC dynamics

### III.3 Network / graph-theoretic signatures

- **Weak edges persistently activated** — amplification recruits low-weight connections that natural SOC would not consistently activate
- **Degree distribution distorted toward high connectivity** — apparent connectivity is elevated
- **Small-world property altered** — high clustering maintained, but path lengths shortened by forced long-range activation
- **Modularity reduced** — module boundaries are more frequently breached by amplified cascades
- **Global efficiency elevated** — but artificially
- **Hub nodes over-recruited** — large high-degree nodes activated in more events than natural SOC predicts; may accumulate structural load

### III.4 Information-theoretic signatures

- **Entropy altered** — state space explored in a biased way, with large coordinated states over-represented
- **Mutual information elevated between subsystems** — but reflecting forced correlation
- **Dynamic range compressed at the upper end** — the system loses discrimination between large inputs
- **Transfer entropy elevated but less specific** — directional and conditional structure distorted
- **Fisher information reduced** despite apparently high sensitivity

### III.5 Dynamical signatures

- **Branching ratio σ persistently above 1** across the observation period
- **σ relatively stationary** — no accumulation-release cycle modulating it (unlike CSOC-like)
- **History-dependent σ modulation** — transient reduction immediately after large events as depletion temporarily reduces propagation, recovering as the system rebuilds
- **Susceptibility persistently elevated** but **history-dependent** — transiently reduced immediately after large events
- **Events more frequent than natural SOC** — continuous elevated activity rather than accumulation-release cycle
- **No quasi-periodicity** — continuous activity rather than long-interval cycle
- **Post-large-event depletion** visible as a measurable history-dependent reduction in subsequent event size or frequency before recovery

### III.6 The unifying pattern

> Amplification of events consistently produces the mirror distortion: **a characteristic scale is introduced where there should be none, but in the opposite direction from CSOC-like — coarse scales over-represented rather than suppressed.**

Both signature bundles destroy the scale-free character of natural SOC, symmetrically — one by truncating the small-event tail, the other by inflating the large-event tail.

## Part IV: Discrimination Criteria

### IV.1 CSOC-like vs. natural SOC

CSOC-like shows all of II.1–II.5. Natural SOC shows none — clean power law across all scales, clean 1/f spectrum, stationary branching ratio, no quasi-periodicity. The clearest single discriminator is the **low-end distribution truncation**: natural SOC produces a clean power law down to the resolution limit; CSOC-like shows truncation above the resolution limit.

### IV.2 CSOC-like vs. subcritical

During the accumulation phase, CSOC-like and subcritical share many surface observables:

| Observable | Subcritical | CSOC-like (accumulation phase) |
|---|---|---|
| Small event frequency | Low | Low (suppressed) |
| Branching ratio | σ < 1 | Appears < 1 |
| Power law | Absent or exponential | Truncated at low end |
| Correlation length | Short | Appears short |
| System activity | Sparse | Sparse |
| Overall appearance | Stable, quiet | Stable, quiet |

The critical differences emerge over longer observation:

| Property | Subcritical | CSOC-like |
|---|---|---|
| Underlying state | True equilibrium below critical point | Underlying SOC dynamics present but masked |
| Stored energy | Not anomalously accumulating | Continuously growing deficit |
| System stability | Genuinely stable | Metastable — coiled |
| Large event distribution | Exponentially suppressed | Excess large events above truncation scale |
| Temporal structure | Stationary | Non-stationary — slow drift in baseline |
| Post-large-event behavior | Returns to same subcritical state | Deep quiescence then resumption of cycle |
| Inter-event intervals | Stationary distribution | Quasi-periodic for large events |
| Baseline measures | Stable | Slow systematic drift between large events |

> **Single-timepoint observation during the accumulation phase cannot reliably distinguish the two. Longitudinal observation across the full cycle is required.**

### IV.3 ISOC-like vs. natural SOC

ISOC-like shows all of III.1–III.5. Natural SOC shows none. The clearest single discriminator is the **history-dependent susceptibility** — natural SOC has stationary response statistics, ISOC-like shows transient post-large-event reduction.

### IV.4 ISOC-like vs. supercritical

ISOC-like and supercritical share surface observables (elevated branching ratio, excess large events, system-spanning cascades, reduced small-event relative frequency). The critical differences:

| Property | Supercritical | ISOC-like |
|---|---|---|
| Underlying state | True state — parameters above critical point | Underlying SOC dynamics present; amplification is external |
| Reversibility | Removing putative amplification does not restore criticality | Removing amplification allows return toward critical |
| Energy source of large events | Internal — natural consequence of supercritical parameters | Mixed — stored potential plus externally added amplification |
| Temporal stationarity | Stationary in supercritical state | Non-stationary — depends on ongoing amplification |
| Post-large-event state | Remains supercritical | Transiently depleted; recovers rapidly |
| Response to perturbation | Uniformly amplified | Amplified but modulated by local depletion state |
| Structural consequences | None implied by parameter state | Potential structural alteration from repeated over-propagation |

> **The key diagnostic is perturbation-response history dependence.** Supercritical responds consistently regardless of recent event history. ISOC-like shows response modulated by local depletion from recent events.

Where the amplification mechanism can be identified and experimentally manipulated, **removing the amplification** is the most definitive test: an ISOC-like system relaxes toward natural SOC; a supercritical system does not.

## Part V: Measurement Procedures

Each measurement protocol below specifies: what to compute, what to expect under natural SOC vs. the distorted categories, and what statistical considerations apply.

### V.1 Distribution analysis

**Procedure:** Estimate the event-size distribution and event-duration distribution over a time window long enough to capture large events. Use the Clauset-Shalizi-Newman (2009) protocol for power-law fitting — maximum-likelihood exponent estimation with goodness-of-fit via bootstrap p-values; compare power-law fit against exponential, log-normal, and truncated alternatives.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — clean power law across the full observable range
- CSOC-like — truncation at low end, excess events above the suppression scale
- ISOC-like — inflation at large end, shallower apparent τ
- Subcritical — exponential decay
- Supercritical — excess at all large scales

### V.2 Exponent consistency testing

**Procedure:** Extract τ (size exponent), α (duration exponent), and the size-duration scaling relation. Check internal consistency via `(α − 1) / (τ − 1) = 1/σνz` (expected scaling relation for the universality class).

**Expected under:** Natural SOC satisfies the relation. Both CSOC-like and ISOC-like violate it, because the distorted distributions break the consistency that holds at true criticality.

### V.3 Avalanche shape collapse

**Procedure:** Extract temporal profiles of events at different sizes. Rescale (size, duration) and attempt shape collapse onto a universal profile.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — clean collapse onto a single profile
- CSOC-like — collapse fails, particularly for large events which reflect deficit-driven dynamics
- ISOC-like — collapse fails, particularly for large events which reflect amplification-sustained dynamics (may show elevated plateaus or extended durations)

### V.4 Spectral analysis

**Procedure:** Compute power spectral density of system activity. Fit to 1/f^β. Examine for deviations, particularly a spectral knee or crossover frequency.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — clean 1/f behavior over the observable range with consistent β
- CSOC-like — knee at high frequency corresponding to the suppression threshold; β inconsistent across frequency ranges
- ISOC-like — steepened or distorted spectrum with excess low-frequency power; opposite direction of CSOC-like

### V.5 Branching-ratio estimation

**Procedure:** Estimate σ from event propagation statistics. For neural-avalanche-style data, use the lag-0 or lag-1 estimator. For time-series data, estimate via ancestor-descendant counting within avalanche-definition windows. Compute σ both globally and within sliding windows to test stationarity.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — σ ≈ 1, stationary
- CSOC-like — σ < 1 during accumulation, > 1 during release; non-stationary
- ISOC-like — σ > 1 across observation, relatively stationary but with history-dependent modulation post-large-event
- Supercritical — σ > 1, stationary, no history dependence

### V.6 Susceptibility measurement

**Procedure:** Apply small perturbations to the system (or examine naturally-occurring small perturbations in observational data) and measure response magnitude as a function of system state and recent event history.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — susceptibility maximized, stationary
- CSOC-like — suppressed during accumulation, spiking immediately before large releases; non-stationary
- ISOC-like — persistently elevated, but history-dependent with transient post-event reduction
- Supercritical — elevated, stationary, no history dependence

### V.7 Temporal structure analysis

**Procedure:** Examine inter-event intervals for events at each size scale. Test for stationarity, quasi-periodicity (via Fourier or autocorrelation of interval time-series), and systematic drift between large events.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — intervals scale-free, no quasi-periodicity
- CSOC-like — quasi-periodic for large events; systematic decrease in small-event frequency during accumulation; deep quiescence post-release
- ISOC-like — continuous elevated activity; no quasi-periodicity; possible short-timescale clustering from rapid reinitiation

### V.8 Network analysis

**Procedure:** Construct functional connectivity graph from co-activation or lagged-correlation data. Compute degree distribution, clustering coefficient, path lengths, modularity, and global efficiency. Examine how these evolve across the cycle.

**Expected under:**
- Natural SOC — scale-free degree, small-world, stable topology
- CSOC-like — progressive loss of weak edges during accumulation, fragmentation toward modularity, spike in global efficiency during large release
- ISOC-like — persistent over-recruitment of weak edges and hub nodes, modularity reduced, global efficiency artificially elevated

### V.9 Cross-domain convergence

**Confirmation criterion for a CSOC-like signature:** truncated distribution + failed shape collapse + failed exponent relations + spectral knee + non-stationary branching ratio + quasi-periodic temporal structure all observed simultaneously and consistently.

**Confirmation criterion for an ISOC-like signature:** inflated large-event distribution + failed shape collapse + failed exponent relations + steepened or distorted spectrum + persistently elevated but history-dependent susceptibility + continuous elevated activity without quasi-periodicity all observed simultaneously and consistently.

Neither category is confirmed by any subset. The standard is convergence.

## Part VI: Co-occurrence Thresholds and Statistical Power

This section specifies what "co-occurrence within statistical tolerance" means operationally. It is presented as a minimum specification; individual studies may tighten thresholds.

### VI.1 Per-signature thresholds

Each individual signature must be measured with a defined statistical standard before it counts as "detected":

- **Power-law fit** — Clauset-Shalizi-Newman goodness-of-fit p ≥ 0.1; preferred model among power-law / exponential / log-normal / truncated via likelihood ratio test with p ≤ 0.05
- **Truncation / inflation scale** — location estimated with 95% bootstrap CI; scale must be above the data resolution floor
- **Exponent relations** — observed exponent within ±0.05 of predicted for the universality class (looser for small datasets)
- **Shape collapse** — χ² goodness-of-fit between rescaled profiles and universal function; threshold calibrated against known-SOC baseline
- **Spectral knee** — two-slope fit preferred over single-slope via BIC/AIC, with knee location within the observable frequency range
- **Branching-ratio non-stationarity** — σ in sliding windows shows variance exceeding natural-SOC bootstrap null by 2σ
- **History dependence** — post-large-event susceptibility measurably different from pre-event baseline (effect size ≥ 0.5, p ≤ 0.05 via matched-pairs test)
- **Quasi-periodicity** — power at a characteristic frequency in interval autocorrelation exceeds natural-SOC null distribution by 2σ

### VI.2 Bundle confirmation thresholds

A signature bundle is confirmed when:

- **All distributional signatures** (V.1–V.3) show the expected deviation simultaneously
- **At least one spectral** (V.4) and **at least one dynamical** (V.5 or V.6) signature shows the expected deviation
- **At least one temporal** (V.7) signature shows the expected deviation
- **Directional consistency** — all detected deviations are in the same direction (all CSOC-like or all ISOC-like, not a mixture)

Mixed signatures (some CSOC-like, some ISOC-like) are not yet categorized by this catalog and require separate treatment.

### VI.3 Sample-size and observation-window requirements

- **Minimum event count** — at least 10³ events spanning at least 3 decades of size; distribution tail tests require ≥ 10² events above the suspected truncation/inflation scale
- **Minimum observation window** — for CSOC-like, at least 3 full accumulation-release cycles (requires observation duration ≥ 3× characteristic period). For ISOC-like, at least 10× the depletion-recovery timescale.
- **Stationarity check** — split the record into halves; signature must replicate in both halves. Fails split-half → record too short or record contains regime change.

### VI.4 Null-model calibration

All statistical thresholds should be calibrated against a **natural-SOC null model** appropriate to the system's universality class (typically Manna or directed percolation for lattice systems). The null defines the expected variability of each signature under the null hypothesis of natural SOC; bundle claims require deviation from this null across multiple signatures simultaneously.

## Appendix: Three-Regime Comparison

| Property | CSOC-like | Natural SOC | ISOC-like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small events | Suppressed | Power law | Amplified |
| Large events | Excess, deficit-driven | Power law | Excess, amplification-driven |
| Branching ratio | < 1 apparent, non-stationary | = 1, stationary | > 1 apparent, history-modulated |
| Resembles | Subcritical | Critical | Supercritical |
| Superficial misclassification | Subcritical | — | Supercritical |
| Temporal structure | Quasi-periodic | Scale-free | Continuous, no accumulation |
| Second timescale | Slow (accumulation) | None | Fast (depletion recovery) |
| Energy deficit | Accumulates | Balanced | Does not accumulate |
| Post-large-event | Deep quiescence | Near-critical return | Rapid recovery |
| Spectral distortion | High-frequency knee | Clean 1/f | Low-frequency excess |
| Distribution distortion | Low-end truncated | None | High-end inflated |
| Exponent relations | Fail | Satisfied | Fail |
| Shape collapse | Fails | Succeeds | Fails |
| Susceptibility | Suppressed, non-stationary | Maximized, stationary | Elevated, history-dependent |
| Structural consequences | From infrequent large releases | None | From continuous over-loading |
