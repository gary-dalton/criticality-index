**15\. Skin in the Game (SITG) as a Signal Regulator**

In a distributed power node system, the absence of centralized privilege requires an alternative mechanism to ensure the "Truthfulness" of the edges. SITG is defined as the **Symmetry of Payoff** ($P$).

### **15.1 The "Cheap Talk" Coefficient ($\\kappa$)**

Without SITG, nodes can transmit "High-Energy" ($E$) instructions (ordering or excitation) without incurring a local cost to their own **Mass (M)**. This is "Cheap Talk."

$$E\_{perceived} \= E\_{true} \\times e^{-\\kappa}$$

* **High $\\kappa$ (No Skin):** The "Edge" becomes a source of **Noise**. The system generates internal energy ($E\_{int}$) because it cannot distinguish between valid stress signals and parasitic rent-seeking.  
* **Low $\\kappa$ (High Skin/Stake):** The "Edge" is **Checksummed**. The signal is high-fidelity because the transmitting node risks its own M (slashing/loss) if the information is false.

### **15.2 SITG vs. Brittle Rigidity**

While SITG ensures truth, **Excessive Stake** creates **Inertial Mass ($M\_{rigid}$)**.

* A node with 100% of its M "Staked" in a specific configuration cannot "Yield" during a fracture.  
* **The Ductility Requirement:** For a "Hellenic" reversion to work, nodes must maintain enough **Liquid Potential (U)** to disconnect from a failing lattice.
